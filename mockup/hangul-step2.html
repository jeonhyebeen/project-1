Korean Greetings: Hello, Thank You, Sorry & Goodbye

Five short phrases cover most of your first day in Korea. Learn which one fits a stranger, a shop owner or a friend, and you will sound polite from the start.

Lesson 1

Hello & goodbye 만남과 헤어짐

Tap ▶ to hear it.

Korean has one “hello” for any time of day. Goodbye is trickier: it changes depending on who walks away.

안녕하세요

Polite

annyeonghaseyo

Hello

Works morning, noon and night. Add a small nod.

안녕

Casual

annyeong

Hi / Bye

Only with close friends or kids. Same word for hello and goodbye.

반가워요

Polite

bangawoyo

Nice to meet you

Say it right after hello when you meet someone new.

또 만나요

Polite

tto mannayo

See you again

A warm way to end a first meeting.

Two kinds of goodbye

Ask yourself one question: who is leaving?

They are leaving

안녕히 가세요

annyeonghi gaseyo

“Go in peace.” Say it to a guest walking out of your shop or home.

You are leaving

안녕히 계세요

annyeonghi gyeseyo

“Stay in peace.” Say it to the person who stays behind.

Lesson 2

Thank you & sorry 감사와 사과

Tap ▶ to hear it.

The long “-ㅂ니다” forms are what you hear from staff and announcements. The shorter “-요” forms are friendly but still polite.

감사합니다

Formal

gamsahamnida

Thank you

The safest choice with anyone you don’t know.

고마워요

Polite

gomawoyo

Thanks

Softer and warmer. Good with coworkers or a friend’s parents.

죄송합니다

Formal

joesonghamnida

I’m sorry

For real apologies, or when you bump into someone.

미안해요

Polite

mianhaeyo

Sorry

Lighter apology between people who know each other.

괜찮아요

Polite

gwaenchanayo

It’s okay / No thanks

The answer to “sorry”, and a polite way to turn something down.

실례합니다

Formal

sillyehamnida

Excuse me

To get past someone or before asking a stranger a question.

Tip: listen for “-요”

If a sentence ends in 요, it is polite. If you drop the 요 with someone older, it can sound rude, so wait until they say 말 놓으세요 (mal noeuseyo, “speak casually with me”).
