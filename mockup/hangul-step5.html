Lesson 1

How to call people 호칭

Tap ▶ to hear it.

Using someone’s first name alone is only for close friends of the same age or younger. With everyone else, add a title.

지민 씨

Jimin ssi

Jimin (polite)

씨 after a name is polite for coworkers and new acquaintances. Never add it to your own name.

선생님

seonsaengnim

Teacher / Sir, Ma’am

님 adds respect. Also used for doctors and many professionals.

선배

seonbae

Senior (school, work)

Someone who started before you. You are their 후배 (hubae).

사장님

sajangnim

Boss / Shop owner

How customers call the owner of a restaurant or shop.

Why Koreans ask your age

몇 살이에요? (myeot sarieyo?, “How old are you?”) is not rude. Age decides who uses polite speech and which title to use, so people ask early.

Lesson 2

The big holidays 명절과 기념일

Two holidays empty out Seoul as families travel home. A few others are just for fun.

설날

Seollal

Jan–Feb

Lunar New Year

Children bow to elders (세배, sebae) and get money (세뱃돈). Everyone eats 떡국, rice cake soup, to turn a year older.

추석

Chuseok

Sep–Oct

Harvest festival

Families visit hometowns, honor ancestors and make half-moon rice cakes called 송편 (songpyeon).

한글날

Hangeullal

Oct 9

Hangul Day

A national holiday celebrating the alphabet you learned in step 1.

빼빼로데이

Ppaeppaero Day

Nov 11

Pepero Day

The date looks like four thin chocolate sticks, so friends and couples trade them.

Lesson 3

Table manners 식사 예절

Tap ▶ to hear it.

Korean meals are shared: many side dishes in the middle, one bowl of rice each. Respect for elders guides everything.

Do

  • ✓Wait for the oldest person to start eating.
  • ✓Pour drinks for others, and hold your glass with two hands when an elder pours for you.
  • ✓Turn your head slightly away when drinking with elders.
  • ✓Leave your rice and soup bowls on the table while you eat.

Don’t

  • ✕Stick your chopsticks upright in rice. It looks like a funeral offering.
  • ✕Pour your own drink at a group dinner.
  • ✕Blow your nose at the table.
  • ✕Start eating before everyone has been served.

Before eating

잘 먹겠습니다

jal meokgesseumnida

“I will eat well.” Thanks to whoever cooked or paid.

After eating

잘 먹었습니다

jal meogeosseumnida

“I ate well.” Say it when you finish or leave.

Lesson 4

Daily life 일상

Small habits you will notice in your first week.

신발

sinbal

Shoes off indoors

In homes and some restaurants with floor seating. Look for a row of shoes at the door.

빨리빨리

ppalli-ppalli

“Quick, quick!”

Fast delivery, fast internet, fast service. Speed is part of daily life.

배달

baedal

Delivery, anytime

Food arrives almost anywhere, even to a park bench along the Han River.

만 나이

man nai

Age, the international way

Since 2023, official age is counted like everywhere else. Older people may still use the traditional count.
