Lesson 1

Ordering at a café 카페에서

Tap ▶ to hear it.

Most café menus in Korea are English loanwords written in Hangul, so you already know half the words.

Listen to the conversation

Staff

어서 오세요.

eoseo oseyo

Welcome.

You

아이스 아메리카노 하나 주세요.

aiseu amerikano hana juseyo

One iced americano, please.

Staff

드시고 가세요?

deusigo gaseyo?

For here?

You

아니요, 포장이요.

aniyo, pojang-iyo

No, to go.

Staff

사천오백 원입니다.

sacheon-obaek wonimnida

That’s 4,500 won.

Pattern

[thing] + 주세요 = “___, please”

  • 물 주세요 mul juseyo · Water, please
  • 이거 주세요 igeo juseyo · This one, please
  • 영수증 주세요 yeongsujeung juseyo · Receipt, please
  • 하나 더 주세요 hana deo juseyo · One more, please

따뜻한

ttatteuthan

hot (drink)

아이스

aiseu

iced

포장

pojang

to go / takeout

매장

maejang

for here (in store)

Lesson 2

Riding the subway 지하철

Tap ▶ to hear it.

Seoul’s subway signs are in Korean and English, but announcements start in Korean. Catch these words and you won’t miss your stop.

Signs you will see

  • 출구

    Exit

    chulgu

  • 입구

    Entrance

    ipgu

  • 갈아타는 곳

    Transfer

    garataneun got

  • 화장실

    Restroom

    hwajangsil

이번 역은 시청역입니다

ibeon yeogeun sicheong-yeogimnida

This stop is City Hall

The announcement you hear before every station.

교통카드

gyotong kadeu

Transit card

T-money cards work on subways, buses and taxis.

몇 번 출구예요?

myeot beon chulguyeyo?

Which exit number?

Locals give directions by exit number.

내려요

Polite

naeryeoyo

I’m getting off

Say it softly to get through a crowded train.

Lesson 3

Paying at a store 가게에서

Tap ▶ to hear it.

Convenience store staff usually ask two questions: do you need a bag, and do you want a receipt. Now you can answer both.

얼마예요?

Polite

eolmayeyo?

How much is it?

카드 돼요?

Polite

kadeu dwaeyo?

Can I pay by card?

Almost always yes. Cards work everywhere.

봉투 필요하세요?

bongtu piryohaseyo?

Do you need a bag?

Bags cost extra. Answer 네 (yes) or 괜찮아요 (no thanks).

영수증 드릴까요?

yeongsujeung deurilkkayo?

Do you want the receipt?

Most people say 버려 주세요 (beoryeo juseyo, “please throw it away”).

Yes and no

네 ne = yes  ·  아니요 aniyo = no. You will also hear 예 (ye), a more formal yes.
