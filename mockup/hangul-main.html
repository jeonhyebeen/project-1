Step 2 · Greetings

Say it like a local

Korean changes with who you talk to. Each phrase is marked Polite for strangers and elders, or Casual for close friends.

안녕하세요

Polite

annyeonghaseyo

Hello. Works any time of day, with anyone.

감사합니다

Polite

gamsahamnida

Thank you. Use with shop staff, teachers, strangers.

고마워

Casual

gomawo

Thanks. Only with friends or younger people.

죄송합니다

Polite

joesonghamnida

I'm sorry. A formal apology.

안녕히 가세요

Polite

annyeonghi gaseyo

Goodbye, said to the person who is leaving.

안녕히 계세요

Polite

annyeonghi gyeseyo

Goodbye, said when you leave and they stay.

    Song lesson preview

    Example line

    우리 같이 걸어요

    uri gachi georeoyo

    “Let's walk together.”

    • 우리

      we

    • 같이

      together

    • 걸어요

      walk (polite)

    Grammar tip: -요 at the end makes a sentence polite. Songs often drop it to sound closer: 걸어.

    Step 5 · Culture

    Why Koreans say it that way

    존댓말

    Honorifics

    Speak politely to elders and strangers. Switch to casual only when invited.

    밥 먹었어요?

    “Did you eat?”

    A friendly way to ask how someone is doing, not a lunch invite.

    설날 · 추석

    The two big holidays

    Lunar New Year and the autumn harvest festival. Families gather and share food.

    두 손으로

    Use two hands

    Give and receive things, and pour drinks for elders, with both hands.

    빨리빨리

    “Hurry, hurry”

    Fast delivery, fast internet, fast everything. A word you'll hear daily.

    신발 벗기

    Shoes off

    Take your shoes off at home and in many traditional restaurants.

    시작해 볼까요?

    sijakhae bolkkayo? · Shall we start?
